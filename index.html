IndyIT brand guide

How IndyIT looks and sounds.

The rules for the IndyIT logo, colours, type and voice, for anyone making something with the IndyIT name on it: Harlan, printers, web developers and sign makers. If something isn't covered here, ask before you guess.

Clear space and minimum size

½ mark½
  • Keep clear space of at least half the mark's height on every side. Nothing else (text, photos, edges) goes inside it.
  • Lockup minimum width: 120 px on screen, 1 inch (25 mm) in print.
  • Mark minimum: 24 px on screen, 0.25 inch (6 mm) in print. Below that, the status dot stops reading.
  • The mark and wordmark stay in this arrangement. Don't stack them, swap them or change the gap.

02

Colour

Five colours. Green does the heavy lifting: Forest for big confident areas, Signal for anything someone can press. Paper keeps it warm. Use no colours outside this set.

How much of each

A typical page or document: mostly Paper and Forest, Signal for calls to action, Mint for highlight bands, Bark for text.

Text and background pairs

SampleText on backgroundContrastUse for

Contrast is measured against the WCAG accessibility standard: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text (24 px and up, or 19 px bold). CMYK values are starting points. Proof before any print run.

04

Voice

IndyIT sounds like a capable engineer who respects your time: direct, confident and approachable. Honest advice beats salesmanship every time.

Principles

  • Plain English. Write for the office manager, not the IT department. Explain the outcome, then the service name.
  • "I" and "we". Use "I" for Harlan's own experience, "we" for what IndyIT does.
  • Practical, not fearful. No scare statistics, countdowns or "it's not if but when".
  • Only real facts. No invented numbers, clients, testimonials or guarantees. Harlan's results from his role as Director of IT at Canal View are always presented as his experience before IndyIT.
  • Cost-conscious. Never position IndyIT as the cheapest. Position it as the partner that won't recommend spending you don't need.

Words we don't use
